## Know your soil
A soil test shows pH, organic matter and key nutrients. It is the best basis for fertiliser and lime decisions. Local extension offices and agricultural laboratories can advise on sampling and interpreting results.

## Build organic matter
Add compost, well-rotted manure and crop residues to improve soil structure, water holding and nutrient supply. Mulching protects soil from heat and erosion. Compost should be allowed to heat and mature properly before use on vegetables.

## Use legumes and rotation
Legumes such as beans, groundnuts and cowpeas fix atmospheric nitrogen and can improve following crops. Rotating crop families breaks cycles of pests and diseases and spreads nutrient demand.

## Balanced fertiliser use
The main nutrients are nitrogen (leafy growth), phosphorus (roots and early growth) and potassium (fruit quality and stress tolerance), plus calcium, magnesium, sulphur and trace elements. Apply what the soil test and crop need, not more. Over-application wastes money and can harm the environment. Splitting nitrogen applications can reduce losses.

## Soil acidity
Many tropical soils are acidic, which limits nutrient availability. Agricultural lime can correct acidity, but the amount must be based on a soil test and local advice.

## Protect the soil
Reduce erosion with contour planting, terraces, cover crops, mulch and minimal soil disturbance. Avoid burning residues where possible, because it removes organic matter. Avoid compaction by not working wet soil.
