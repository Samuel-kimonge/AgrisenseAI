## Water
Clean water must always be available. Cattle drink large amounts, and lactating cows need more. Poor water supply reduces feed intake and milk yield.

## Forage first
Good quality pasture, hay, silage and fodder crops make up most of a cow's diet. Forage quality and quantity change with season, so plan feed reserves for the dry season. Chopping and wilting fodder, and avoiding mouldy feed, reduces waste and illness.

## Energy, protein and minerals
Lactating cows and growing animals need more energy and protein than maintenance animals. Supplement based on forage quality and production level. Provide a suitable mineral supplement or salt lick, because many soils and forages lack enough minerals. A veterinarian or livestock officer can help balance a ration for your animals and feed.

## Changes in feed
Change feeds gradually over several days. Sudden changes, especially to rich feeds or lush legume pasture, can cause bloat and digestive upset.

## Calves
Newborn calves should receive colostrum, the first milk, as soon as possible after birth, within the first hours, because it provides antibodies. Feed clean milk or milk replacer in clean containers, and introduce clean water and starter feed and good forage gradually as advised by your veterinarian or livestock officer.

## Feed safety
Do not feed mouldy hay, silage or grain. Keep feed stores dry. Keep animals away from toxic plants and from fertiliser, urea and chemical stores.
