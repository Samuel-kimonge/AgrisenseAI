## Starting with a healthy crop
Use certified, disease-free seed or healthy transplants from a reputable nursery. Raise seedlings in a clean seedbed or trays with clean media, and protect nurseries with insect-proof net to keep out whiteflies and aphids, which spread viruses. Avoid planting tomatoes in a field where tomato, potato, pepper or eggplant grew recently. Rotate with non-solanaceous crops such as maize, beans or brassicas for two to three seasons.

## Spacing, staking and pruning
Give plants enough space so leaves dry quickly after rain or dew. Stake or trellis plants to keep foliage and fruit off the soil. Remove the lowest leaves that touch the ground and prune as recommended for your variety so air can move through the canopy. Good airflow lowers humidity around leaves, which reduces many fungal diseases such as early blight, leaf mold and Septoria leaf spot.

## Water management
Water at the base of the plant, in the morning, so leaves are dry by evening. Drip irrigation or careful furrow irrigation reduces splash that spreads leaf spot diseases. Irregular watering can cause blossom-end rot and fruit cracking, so keep soil moisture even. Mulching reduces splash and helps keep moisture steady.

## Nutrition
Tomatoes need balanced nutrition. Too little nitrogen weakens plants and can make leaf spots worse; too much produces soft, leafy growth that is more attractive to pests and diseases. Calcium supply and steady watering help prevent blossom-end rot. Use a soil test and local extension recommendations to set fertiliser types and amounts rather than guessing.

## Scouting and early action
Walk the field at least once a week. Check the older leaves, the undersides of leaves, stems and fruit. Early detection lets you remove a few infected leaves or plants before the problem spreads. Write down what you see and when. Take clear photos for expert advice.

## Hygiene
Remove and destroy diseased plant material instead of composting it. Clean tools between plants and fields. Wash hands before handling plants, especially after handling tobacco products, which can carry tomato mosaic virus. Avoid working in the crop when leaves are wet. Remove weeds, because some host pests and viruses.

## Getting local advice on chemicals
Which fungicides, bactericides or insecticides are registered and suitable depends on your country and the crop stage. This guide does not list products or rates. Ask an extension officer or a licensed agro-input dealer, follow the label exactly, wear protective equipment and observe the pre-harvest interval.
