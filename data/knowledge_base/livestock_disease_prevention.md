## Herd health plan
Work with a veterinarian or animal health worker to make a herd health plan for your area. It usually covers vaccination, parasite control, nutrition, housing and record keeping. Which vaccinations are appropriate depends on local disease risks and regulations, so ask your veterinarian.

## Biosecurity
Isolate newly bought animals and animals returning from shows or markets for a period advised by your veterinarian before mixing them with the herd. Buy from sources you trust and, where possible, ask about the herd's health history. Limit visitors, clean boots and equipment, and keep sick animals separate. Avoid sharing equipment between farms without cleaning.

## Ticks and flies
Tick-borne diseases and biting flies are major problems in many regions. Ask your veterinarian or animal health worker about a tick control programme suited to your area. Reduce fly breeding by managing manure and standing water.

## Worms and other parasites
Use dung tests and veterinary advice to decide when to treat for worms rather than treating routinely without testing, which encourages drug resistance.

## Hygiene in milking and housing
Keep housing clean, dry and well ventilated. Wash udders and hands before milking, use clean equipment, and milk affected animals last. Good milking hygiene reduces mastitis.

## Disposal of dead animals
Dispose of carcasses according to veterinary and local regulations. Do not drag or open a carcass of an animal that died suddenly.

## Diseases that can infect people
Some animal diseases, such as brucellosis, anthrax, rabies and ringworm, can infect people. Avoid raw milk unless it is known to be safe, wear gloves when handling birth material or sick animals, and wash hands afterward.
