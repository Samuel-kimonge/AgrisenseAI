## Seed and planting
Choose a variety recommended for your altitude, rainfall and growing season by local seed suppliers or extension services. Use certified seed. Plant at the start of reliable rains into a well-prepared seedbed. Spacing is commonly about 75 cm between rows and 25 to 30 cm between plants, but follow the recommendation that comes with your seed and local extension advice.

## Weeding
Weeds compete strongly with young maize. Weed early and keep the crop clean in the first several weeks after emergence, then control later flushes. Early weeding often protects yield more than later weeding.

## Fertility
Use a soil test and local recommendations to choose fertiliser and manure. Combining organic matter (manure or compost) with appropriate mineral fertiliser often improves results on poor soils. Rotating maize with legumes such as beans or groundnuts helps soil fertility. This guide does not give fertiliser rates because they depend on soil, variety, rainfall and local recommendations.

## Common problems
Fall armyworm damages leaves and the central whorl, leaving ragged holes and sawdust-like droppings; scout often from early growth and ask extension officers about current local control advice. Stalk borers tunnel in stems. Leaf diseases such as grey leaf spot and northern leaf blight are favoured by humid weather and by maize residue left in fields. Maize lethal necrosis, a viral disease, is reduced by using clean seed, rotating crops, controlling volunteer maize and managing insects. Remove and destroy badly affected plants where advised.

## Harvest and storage
Harvest when grain is mature and dry the cobs or grain quickly. Store grain only when it is dry enough, commonly around 13.5 percent moisture, in clean, dry, ventilated stores. Mouldy grain can contain aflatoxin, which is harmful to people and animals, so discard mouldy grain rather than feeding or selling it.
