## The IPM approach
Integrated pest management combines several methods so that chemicals are a last resort rather than the first response. The steps are prevent, monitor, identify, act and review.

## Prevent
Use healthy seed, resistant or tolerant varieties, crop rotation, field hygiene, correct spacing, balanced nutrition and good water management. Remove weeds and crop residues that shelter pests and pathogens.

## Monitor and identify
Scout regularly and record what you find. Identify the problem correctly before acting: fungal, bacterial, viral, insect and nutrient problems can look similar but need different responses. Mite damage, virus symptoms and nutrient deficiencies are often confused with fungal diseases.

## Act
Start with the least disruptive options: remove infested or infected plants or leaves, use physical barriers such as nets, use traps, and encourage natural enemies. When a chemical is needed, choose a product that is registered for the crop and pest in your country, apply it exactly as the label directs, and respect the pre-harvest interval. Rotate chemical groups as advised to slow resistance. Broad-spectrum insecticides kill beneficial insects and can make pest outbreaks worse.

## Safety
Wear the protective equipment listed on the label. Keep chemicals in original, labelled containers away from children, food and feed. Never reuse empty pesticide containers for food or water. Do not spray near water sources or when bees are foraging.

## Get local advice
Recommended products and rates change and differ by country. An extension officer or licensed agro-dealer can advise on current local options.
